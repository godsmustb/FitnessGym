# Fitness Companion App - Literature Survey (2022-2026 priority + classics)

Compiled 2026-10-04 via web search. Method note: findings come from search-result abstracts/snippets and a few page fetches (several publisher pages were cookie-walled, so full texts were NOT read). Every entry below was surfaced by a search; nothing is from memory alone. Anything I could not confirm is tagged **(unverified)**. Effect sizes quoted are as reported in abstracts/secondary summaries. Items with arXiv IDs of the form 26xx.xxxxx were returned by search but not independently opened - treat as **(unverified)** preprints.

Product focus: equipment-aware custom workout generation; animated form + muscle-target visuals; daily rep/set/weight logging; progression; adherence.

## 1. Resistance-training programming evidence

| # | Paper | Venue / year | Link | Key finding | What it means for our app |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC. *The Resistance Training Dose-Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gain* | Sports Medicine, 2025 (preprint 2024) | doi:10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w ; https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460 | 67 studies / 2,058 participants. Volume-hypertrophy slope positive with diminishing returns (a secondary summary reports no detectable benefit beyond ~31 "fractional" sets/wk **(unverified figure)**); strength shows far more pronounced diminishing returns. Frequency: negligible effect on hypertrophy, positive (diminishing) effect on strength. Counting indirect sets as 0.5 fits best. | Generator should count **fractional sets** (e.g. bench = 1 chest + 0.5 triceps/front delts) when budgeting weekly volume per muscle. Frequency is a scheduling convenience for hypertrophy, matters a bit more for strength goals. |
| 1.2 | Remmert JF, Pelland JC, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC. *Is There Too Much of a Good Thing? | 1.3 | Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW. *Dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and increases in muscle mass: a systematic review and meta-analysis* | J Sports Sci, 2017;35:1073-82 | doi:10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197 (DOI unverified) ; semantic scholar link found | 15 studies, 34 groups. | 1.4 | Baz-Valle E, Fontes-Villalba M, Santos-Concejero J. *A systematic review of the effects of different resistance training volumes on muscle hypertrophy* | J Hum Kinet, 2022 (PMC8884877) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884877/ | 12-20 weekly sets/muscle is a reasonable standard for trained young men; >20 may help triceps. | Default band 12-20 for intermediate users. |

| 1.6 | Krieger JW. *Single vs. multiple sets of resistance exercise for muscle hypertrophy: a meta-analysis* | J Strength Cond Res, 2010 | ovid/semantic scholar links found | Multiple sets ~ 40% greater hypertrophy ES than 1 set. | Beginners: 1-3 sets already effective; progress to multiple sets. |
| 1.7 | Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW. *Effects of resistance training frequency on measures of muscle hypertrophy: a systematic review and meta-analysis* | Sports Med, 2016;46:1689-97 | doi:10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8 | Training a muscle >=2x/wk beat 1x/wk (volume-equated); 3x vs 2x undetermined. | 1.8 | Refalo MC, Helms ER, Trexler ET, Hamilton DL, Fyfe JJ. *Influence of resistance training proximity-to-failure on skeletal muscle hypertrophy: a systematic review with meta-analysis* | Sports Med, 2023;53:649-665 | doi:10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y | 15 studies; small edge for sets closer to failure (ES ~0.15-0.21), larger when loads <=50% 1RM; failure vs non-failure not clearly different otherwise. | Target 0-3 RIR; allow ~0-1 RIR on isolation/low-fatigue moves; avoid mandating failure on compounds. |
| 1.9 | Helms ER, Cronin J, Storey A, Zourdos MC. *Application of the repetitions-in-reserve-based RPE scale for resistance training* | Strength Cond J, 2016 | PMC4961270 | Introduces RIR-based RPE for autoregulating load. | Use RIR as the in-app effort input; show 0-4 RIR picker with plain-language anchors. |

| 1.12 | Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Van Every DW, Plotkin DL. *Loading recommendations for muscle strength, hypertrophy, and local endurance: a re-examination of the repetition continuum* | Sports, 2021;9(2):32 | https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4663/9/2/32 | Strength favors heavy loads; hypertrophy across wide spectrum; endurance favors light. | Same as above; supports goal-specific rep ranges, not rigid "8-12". |
| 1.13 | Plotkin D, Coleman M, Van Every D, Maldonado J, Oberlin D, Israetel M, Feather J, Alto A, Vigotsky AD, Schoenfeld BJ. *Progressive overload without progressing load? | 1.16 | Grgic J, Schoenfeld BJ, Skrepnik M, Davies TB, Mikulic P. *Effects of rest interval duration in resistance training on measures of muscular strength: a systematic review* | Sports Med, 2018 | PubMed 28933024 | Strength gains possible even with <60 s; >2 min maximizes strength in trained; 60-120 s fine for novices. | Rest defaults by level and goal. |
| 1.17 | Schoenfeld BJ, Pope ZK, et al. *Longer interset rest periods enhance muscle strength and hypertrophy in resistance-trained men* | J Strength Cond Res, 2016 | journals.lww.com link found | 3 min rest > 1 min for strength and quad hypertrophy (volume load confound). | Supports >=2 min on heavy lower-body compounds. |
| 1.18 | Schoenfeld BJ, Fisher J, Grgic J, Haun C, Helms E, Phillips S, Steele J, Vigotsky A. *Resistance training recommendations to maximize muscle hypertrophy in an athletic population: position stand of the IUSCA* | Int J Strength Cond, 2021;1(1) | doi:10.47206/ijsc.v1i1.81 | Consensus-style practical guidance (volume, load, failure, rest). | Good single reference for the "defaults" table. |
| 1.19 | Rogerson D / Coleman M / Hecksteden group - *Gaining more from doing less? Effects of a one-week deload during supervised RT on muscular adaptations* | PeerJ, 2024 (PeerJ 16777); author list **(unverified)** | https://peerj.com/articles/16777.pdf | 1-wk deload mid 9-wk block: no meaningful difference in muscle size; continuous training gave greater lower-body strength gains. | Do NOT hard-schedule deloads; make them **symptom/performance-triggered** (stalled reps, high RIR-adjusted fatigue, user-requested). |
| 1.20 | *Resistance training prescription during planned deloading periods: survey of S&C coaches* | 2024 (PubMed 39446750); authors unverified | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39446750/ | Describes practice (not efficacy) of deloads. | Deload evidence is thin: low-confidence default. |
| 1.21 | Soslu R et al. and others - velocity-based vs percentage-based training meta-analyses | Multiple, 2022-2026 (e.g., BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil 2025; doi:10.1177/17479541261452541) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12870409/ | VBT roughly equals %1RM for maximal strength; small edges for jump/COD. | Velocity-based autoregulation is optional/advanced; RIR/RPE suffices for general users. Phone-camera bar velocity is a possible premium feature. |
| 1.22 | Androulakis-Korakakis P et al. (minimal-dose / beginner work, incl. *The minimum effective training dose required to increase 1RM strength in powerlifters*) | Solent Univ.; Eur J Sport Sci | https://pure.solent.ac.uk/ws/files/24506816/The_minimum_effective_training_dose_for_1RM_strength.pdf | Very low doses (~1 set of ~6-15 reps, 1 day/wk) still improve strength in untrained/hesitant lifters. | Offer a "minimal" plan tier (2-3 sessions/wk, 1-2 sets/exercise) for beginners and low-time users. |

**Beginner vs advanced synthesis:** novices respond to low volume (single sets of 8-15, not to failure) and gains saturate quickly; trained lifters need more volume and nearer-failure effort (1.3, 1.4, 1.8, 1.22). This justifies level-dependent defaults.

**Synthesis:** evidence says LLM plans are plausible but inconsistent and weak on progression/safety. A **constraint-based generator** (muscle-volume targets x equipment filter x time budget x injury flags) with LLM only for explanation is best supported. I found no published head-to-head of constraint planners vs LLMs on hypertrophy outcomes (gap G1).

# (a) Evidence-based defaults for the generator

Confidence: H = multiple meta-analyses; M = single meta-analysis or narrow RCT base; L = practice/consensus or thin evidence. These are defaults to be overridden by user goal, schedule, and recovery signals; they are not medical advice.

| Parameter | Beginner (<~6-12 mo) | Intermediate | Advanced | Supporting papers | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly sets per muscle (direct sets; count indirect sets at 0.5) | 4-8 (minimal tier 2-4) | 10-16 | 12-20 (up to ~20+ for lagging muscles; plateau near ~30 fractional) | 1.1, 1.3, 1.4, 1.6, 1.22 | M-H |
| Sets per muscle per session | <=6 | <=8 | <=10-11 fractional | 1.2 | M |
| Frequency per muscle | 2x (1x minimal) | 2x (3x if strength goal) | 2-3x | 1.1, 1.7, 1.14 | M |
| Rep range (hypertrophy) | 8-15 | 6-20 (exercise-dependent) | 5-30 near failure; use light for isolation | 1.11, 1.12 | H |
| Rep range (strength emphasis) | 5-10 | 3-8 | 1-6 for main lifts | 1.11, 1.12, 1.14 | H |
| Proximity to failure (RIR) | 2-4 RIR, no failure | 1-3 RIR; 0-1 on isolation last set | 0-2 RIR; occasional failure on low-risk lifts | 1.8, 1.9, 1.10, 7.6 | M |
| Rest between sets | 60-120 s | 90 s isolation; 2-3 min compounds | 2-3+ min heavy compounds | 1.15, 1.16, 1.17 | M |
| Progression rule | Double progression: add reps to top of range at target RIR, then +smallest load step | Same + RIR-driven load adjustment | Same + microloading / set-add mesocycle | 1.13, 1.9 | M |
| Load adjust trigger | Hit top reps on all sets at <=target RIR for 1-2 sessions -> +2.5-5% (upper) / +5-10% (lower) (rule of thumb **L**) | Same | Smaller steps | 1.13 | L |
| Volume progression within block | Hold volume ~4-6 wks, add sets only if recovering well | Add 1-2 sets/muscle every 1-2 wks over 4-6 wk block | Same; cap at plateau | 1.1, 1.2 (Haun 2018, Schoenfeld 2019 RCTs for volume-response) | M |
| Deload | Not scheduled | Reactive only: when e-1RM/reps drop for 2 sessions or user reports fatigue/pain | Reactive; optionally every 6-10 wks | 1.19, 1.20 (evidence thin) | L |
| Tempo / ROM | Controlled 2-3 s eccentric, full comfortable ROM | Emphasize long-muscle-length position | Include lengthened partials as option | 2.1-2.5 | M |
| Exercise selection | Machines/bodyweight/DB all fine; 1-2 per muscle | 2-3 per muscle, include a lengthened-position variant | Rotate variants | 2.1, 2.6-2.9, 2.12 | M |
| Equipment substitution | Swap within movement pattern; keep set-count and RIR | same | For strength goal keep the competition-like lift | 2.6, 2.7, 2.8, 2.9 | M |
| Cue style | External cues for technique; internal "feel the muscle" on light isolation sets | same | same | 5.3, 5.5, 5.6 | M |
| Screening | PAR-Q+ at onboarding, referral flow on positives | PAR-Q+ yearly | PAR-Q+ yearly | 7.3, 7.4 | H |
| Onboarding / habit design | 12-week arc; forgiving streaks; minimal session option | - | - | 6.1, 6.11, 6.14 | M |

Typical weekly default (3 sessions, whole-body, beginner) = ~6 sets per major muscle across 3 days, giving the dose-response benefit while keeping sessions short.

# (b) Open research gaps we could exploit

- **G1. No head-to-head of constraint-based planners vs LLM vs coach plans on outcomes** (hypertrophy/adherence). Papers 3.1-3.6 evaluate quality by expert rating only; the scoping review (3.5) calls current evaluation weak (median rigor 2.5/5). Publishable: an open evaluation harness with automated safety/volume validators.
- **G2. Phone-based form feedback has no injury-prevention or learning-outcome trials**; only accuracy/validity studies (4.2, 4.11). An RCT or large observational study on cue acceptance and technique change would be novel.
- **G3. RIR calibration for novices**: RIR accuracy improves with experience (1.10, 7.6). A bias-correcting estimator that uses logged load/reps and bar speed could improve autoregulation; not found in the literature surveyed.
- **G4. Animated vs static vs video demos for lifting technique in-app**: general animation meta-analysis supports it (5.1) but no gym-specific trial found.
- **G5. Deload efficacy** is thin (1.19, 1.20); adaptive, data-triggered deloads vs continuous training is open.
- **G6. Internal vs external focus tension** (5.3 vs 5.5/5.6): which cue by exercise/load/experience in app delivery has not been tested at scale.
- **G7. Equipment-constrained programming** (home with bands/DBs only): evidence on equivalence exists per modality (2.6-2.9) but not on *generated whole programs* under equipment constraints.
- **G8. Fractional-set volume accounting in consumer apps**: 1.1 gives a method; I did not find it implemented/validated in an app.
- **G9. JITAI for strength training** (timing + type of nudge from workout logs): HeartSteps (3.8, 6.10) is walking-focused; resistance-training JITAIs not found.
- **G10. Women, older adults, and non-lab populations are under-represented** in much of the volume/frequency evidence (stated in several sources; I did not quantify) - opportunity for sex/age-stratified defaults from app data (with consent).
